## 1. ER diagram — what the data looks like
- The system's central entity is **Order** (alongside **Product** and **CatalogVendor**), because **Order** anchors the commercial workflow—connecting buyers (`User` or anonymous walk-ins), sellers (`CatalogVendor`), purchased items (`OrderItem`), inventory deductions (`InventoryMovement`), linked commercial records (`Transaction`), and vendor POS records (`VendorTx`).
- An Order belongs to exactly **one** vendor(s), because the `vendor` foreign key on `Order` is `NOT NULL` and a multi-vendor checkout splits the cart into one separate `Order` per vendor (`OrderListCreateView.post`), each with its own `Transaction`, `Payment`, and (if applicable) `DeliveryBooking`.
- Order, Transaction and Payment are three separate tables because each owns a single authority for a distinct fact without duplicating state: `Order` is the order entity holding the customer-facing lifecycle (`Order.status`), `Transaction` is a linked commercial record (`reference_no` and `transaction_type` separating `ONLINE_ORDER`, `PICKUP_RESERVATION`, and `POS_WALKIN` without carrying its own status), and `Payment` records individual payment attempts and settlement outcomes (`Payment.status`, enforcing idempotency via `unique_together (provider, provider_reference)` and unifying digital gateways with `CASH` receipts).
- The lookup tables (`TransactionStatus`, `PaymentStatus`, **`DeliveryStatus`, `TransactionType`, `FulfillmentMethod`, `AvailabilityStatus`, `InventoryReason`**) exist so that **valid domain states, categories, and operational metadata (such as `sort_order`, `is_terminal`, `is_orderable`, and inventory `direction`) are strictly standardized and seeded at the database level rather than relying on arbitrary strings or guessing states from unrelated columns**.
- `ComparisonGroup` and `UnitOfMeasure` exist so that **products priced in different units (such as pieces versus kilograms) can be normalized to a common `base_unit` via `ComparisonGroupUnit.factor_to_base` (e.g., `1 pc = 0.25 kg` $\rightarrow$ `factor_to_base = 0.25`), enabling customers to compare equivalent prices across vendors**.

## 2. Class diagram — the same model as objects
- The class diagram and the ER diagram describe **the exact same domain data model (Revision 2, mirroring each other entity-for-entity and relationship-for-relationship), represented respectively as Django ORM domain classes and relational database tables**.
- Django models are thin data holders; the business logic lives in **`apps/orders/services.py` and the `APIView` classes (`apps/*/views.py`)**.

## 3. Sequence diagram — how the calls flow
- Placing an order splits the cart because **each `Order` is strictly scoped to a single `CatalogVendor` (`vendor` is `NOT NULL`), so items grouped by vendor during checkout must generate one independent `Order`, `Transaction`, `Payment`, and `DeliveryBooking` per vendor**.
- Payment is verified server-side because **a client-side redirect alone is not proof of payment; querying the Payment Gateway directly from `Order / Payment Service` prevents spoofed confirmations, ensures `Order.status` only advances from `PENDING_PAYMENT` to `CONFIRMED` after real settlement (`Payment.status = VERIFIED`), and prevents double charges alongside idempotency checks**.
- The vendor POS flow is vendor-scoped because **a vendor (`catalog.Vendor`) must only view and act on orders they personally fulfill (`WHERE vendor = me`), ensuring store isolation and enforcing transition guards against `Order.status` strictly within their own storefront**.

## 4. Activity diagram — the decisions
- The checkout flow rejects an invalid cart because **orders and transactions cannot be created if vendor status, item quantities, product availability, stock levels, or prices fail validation—preventing unfulfillable orders and ensuring the user receives a correction message with zero database records created**.
- The pickup flow takes cash at the stall, which means **taking cash is recorded strictly as a payment event (`Payment` with `provider = CASH`, `status = VERIFIED`) that calculates change due (`cash - total_amount`) and generates an auditable BIR-style 72mm receipt serial (`RC-{reference_no}-{receipt_attempt}`), while leaving `Order.status` unchanged until the vendor explicitly executes `Confirm Pickup` (`POST /confirm-pickup/`) to transition the order to `COMPLETED`**.

## 5. Use case diagram — who can do what
- The actors are **Customer, Vendor, Market Administrator, Payment Gateway, and Delivery Provider**.
- A Customer can **register/sign in, browse/search/filter products, view product details, compare equivalent prices, view the interactive market map, manage their shopping cart, choose pickup or delivery, validate cart/prices/availability, submit pickup reservations, place paid orders, initiate/verify online payments, quote/book delivery, view order/payment/delivery status, manage favorites, and view order history/quick reorder**; a Vendor can **sign in, manage their own products and inventory, update order status, record cash payments, print receipts, confirm pickups, mark orders as out for delivery or delivered, and view their sales dashboard**; an Administrator can **oversee vendors and products and correct transaction statuses**.
